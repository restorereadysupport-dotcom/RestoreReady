Backup & Restore Readiness Assessment

A sample report demonstrating our assessment approach and practical recommendations.

Important: Fictional sample. All organisation details, observations and findings below are illustrative. This is not a real client assessment or verified technical evidence.

1. Executive Summary

Example organisation: Northstar Manufacturing (fictional)
Example review period: 30 days
Example environment: Virtual machines, file services and business applications.

This illustrative assessment highlights the importance of validating recovery, investigating recurring backup failures and reviewing protection of backup copies. Successful backup jobs alone do not prove that business services can be recovered within required timeframes.

Example overall status: Needs improvement — subject to evidence validation and recovery testing.

2. Assessment Scope

  • Review backup policies, schedules, retention and workload coverage.
  • Examine backup job history, failures and alerting.
  • Review available restore-test evidence and recovery procedures.
  • Evaluate evidence of backup isolation, immutability and privileged access controls.
  • Identify risks and recommend prioritised remediation actions.

A real assessment would have an agreed scope, access requirements, evidence sources and documented exclusions.

3. Sample Findings Register

ID / PriorityIllustrative findingRecommended action
F-01 — HIGHRecent restore evidence is incomplete for a critical application.Conduct an authorised test restore with the application owner. Record integrity checks, recovery time and sign-off.
F-02 — HIGHRecurring backup failures have not been reviewed through a documented trend analysis.Identify root causes, assign ticket owners and verify successful follow-up jobs.
F-03 — HIGHEvidence of backup isolation and recovery access controls is incomplete.Validate applicable immutability, access restrictions, separation of duties and recovery credentials.
F-04 — MEDIUMRetention settings are not clearly mapped to business requirements.Document retention requirements by workload and obtain approval for exceptions.
F-05 — MEDIUMThe recovery runbook lacks some application dependencies and ownership details.Update recovery order, prerequisites, contacts, validation steps and escalation procedures.

4. Prioritised Remediation Plan

Suggested timeframeActionClosure evidence
Days 0–7Plan a recovery test for critical workloads.Approved test plan and documented results.
Days 0–7Investigate recurring backup failures.Root-cause notes, assigned actions and successful validation jobs.
Weeks 1–2Review backup isolation and privileged access.Completed control checklist and tracked remediation actions.
Weeks 2–4Align retention policies and update recovery documentation.Approved retention matrix and reviewed runbook.

These are illustrative timeframes. Actual priorities depend on business impact, evidence and operational constraints.

5. Evidence Checklist

  • Backup policy and protected-workload reports.
  • Job history, failure records and monitoring alerts.
  • Approved retention requirements and exceptions.
  • Authorised restore-test records and validation results.
  • Backup architecture, isolation and access-control evidence.
  • Current recovery runbooks and BCP/DR exercise records.

Sensitive credentials, passwords, tokens and production secrets should never be included in the report.

6. Conclusion

A useful backup assessment connects backup configuration and job health to demonstrable recovery outcomes. Findings should be evidence-based, prioritised by business impact, assigned to accountable owners and closed only after agreed validation.
