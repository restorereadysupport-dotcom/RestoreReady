4. Key Recovery Readiness Findings

RR-01 · Recovery testing HIGH PRIORITY

Critical application recovery has not been sufficiently validated

Illustrative observation: Available example evidence does not demonstrate a recent end-to-end recovery exercise for a critical business application.

Potential impact: Actual recovery time, data integrity and application availability may differ from expectations during a major incident.

Recommended action: Plan an authorised recovery exercise with infrastructure, database and application owners. Record recovery duration, validation results and business sign-off.

RR-02 · Application dependencies HIGH PRIORITY

Recovery sequence and dependencies require clarification

Illustrative observation: The example recovery documentation does not consistently map application dependencies, databases, authentication services and network prerequisites.

Potential impact: Individual components may be restored successfully while the complete business service remains unavailable.

Recommended action: Document application recovery order, upstream and downstream dependencies, validation checkpoints and accountable owners.

RR-03 · Recovery environment HIGH PRIORITY

Recovery capacity and access readiness need validation

Illustrative observation: Evidence of available compute, storage, network capacity, cloud quotas and emergency access is incomplete.

Potential impact: Recovery may be delayed if the required resources or authorised access are unavailable when needed.

Recommended action: Validate recovery capacity and access arrangements against the critical workload inventory. Confirm that required resources can be made available within agreed recovery objectives.

RR-04 · Recovery governance MEDIUM PRIORITY

Recovery responsibilities and escalation paths need strengthening

Illustrative observation: The example plan does not clearly identify all technical and business decision-makers during a recovery event.

Potential impact: Delayed decisions, unclear ownership and coordination gaps may extend service disruption.

Recommended action: Establish a recovery responsibility matrix, escalation contacts and business validation responsibilities. Review them during scheduled exercises.

5. Prioritised Improvement Roadmap

The following example roadmap sequences improvements by urgency and practical execution.

Timeframe Recommended activity Expected evidence of completion
0–30 days Identify critical applications and confirm business-approved RTO and RPO requirements. Approved critical workload inventory and recovery objectives.
0–30 days Review recovery runbooks, application dependencies and recovery ownership. Updated recovery procedures, dependency map and responsibility matrix.
30–60 days Conduct controlled recovery tests for selected critical workloads. Test results, actual recovery duration, data validation and application-owner sign-off.
30–60 days Validate recovery infrastructure capacity, access and prerequisites. Documented capacity and access validation with identified remediation items.
60–90 days Conduct a coordinated recovery exercise and track improvement actions. Exercise report, lessons learned, assigned owners and closure tracking.

6. Evidence to Validate During a Real Assessment

A real engagement would validate observations using agreed evidence sources and authorised discussions. Examples include:

  • Business-approved RTO and RPO requirements.
  • Critical application and infrastructure dependency maps.
  • Recovery runbooks and escalation procedures.
  • Recent restore and application recovery test records.
  • Recovery infrastructure capacity and access validation.
  • Disaster recovery exercise reports and tracked corrective actions.
  • Named technical owners and business stakeholders.

The scope and availability of evidence will vary by organisation. Findings should be confirmed before they are treated as actual risks or control gaps.

7. Suggested Success Measures

Measure What it demonstrates
Recovery test completion Whether planned recovery exercises are performed and documented.
Actual recovery time versus RTO Whether tested recovery performance meets business expectations.
Data recovery point versus RPO Whether recovered data meets agreed data-loss tolerances.
Runbook and dependency coverage Whether recovery procedures account for critical service dependencies.
Corrective action closure Whether issues identified during tests and exercises are resolved.

8. Closing Perspective

Recovery readiness is demonstrated through preparation, tested procedures, available resources and coordinated execution. It should not be judged solely by the presence of successful backup jobs.

A structured assessment helps organisations understand where recovery capability is strong, where evidence is missing and which improvements should be prioritised first.
